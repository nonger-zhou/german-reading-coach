Ministers rule out early election despite mounting pressure

The government has ruled out calling an early election, even as pressure mounts on the prime minister following a string of damaging by-election defeats. Speaking in Parliament on Tuesday, Keir Starmer conceded that the results were disappointing but insisted the party would not abandon its long-term plan.

Critics argue that the strategy has undermined public trust. Opposition leaders accused ministers of clinging to power and called for a vote of no confidence. "The country deserves better than a government that keeps kicking the can down the road," one senior MP said.

Analysts say the setbacks reflect deeper discontent over the cost of living. Inflation has eased, albeit slowly, yet household budgets remain under strain. Several economists warned that the latest spending cuts could exacerbate regional inequality, particularly in former industrial towns.

Nonetheless, the Treasury maintains that its fiscal rules are sound. Officials pointed to a modest rebound in business investment and said the reforms would bear fruit within two years. Unprecedented levels of borrowing during the pandemic, they added, left little room for manoeuvre.

Behind the scenes, however, some backbenchers are reportedly drawing up contingency plans. Whether the leadership can weather the storm may depend on how quickly the economy picks up over the coming months.
